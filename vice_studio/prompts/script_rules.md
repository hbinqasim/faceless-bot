# Universal Script Writing Rules

These rules apply to all Vice Studio script-writing agents. Follow these guidelines for consistent, engaging, accurate, and platform-optimized narration.

---

## Hook & Opening (First 3 Seconds)

- **Grab attention within first sentence** - hook is non-negotiable, viewer decides in 1-2 seconds whether to continue
- **Establish why viewer should care** - what's at stake, what's surprising, what's important
- **Create curiosity or emotional response** - pose a question, reveal a secret, trigger emotion
- **Avoid slow build-ups** - no lengthy context-setting before engagement
- **Strong opening statement** - clear, direct, compelling first line
- **Examples of hooks**:
  - Question hook: "Did you know [surprising fact]?"
  - Statement hook: "[Shocking statement that demands explanation]"
  - Pattern interrupt: "Stop thinking about [misconception]..."
  - Emotional hook: "This is how [character] changed everything..."

---

## Factual Accuracy & Sourcing

- **Verify all claims before inclusion** - fact-check against official sources
- **Source from official channels only** - Rockstar Newswire, official trailers, verified announcements
- **Clearly label speculation or theory as such** - "Rumored to..." or "Fans speculate that..." if including non-confirmed info (minimal)
- **No misleading or false information** - don't present theories as facts
- **No fabricated details** - every claim must be defensible
- **Reference knowledge base** - use `vice_studio/knowledge/[channel].md` for verified facts
- **Acknowledge uncertainty** - "It's unclear..." or "Evidence suggests..." for ambiguous topics

---

## Concise Narration

- **Every word serves the story** - no filler, no repetition, no tangents
- **Remove redundancy** - say it once, say it clearly, move on
- **Direct and conversational tone** - natural speech, not stiff or overly formal
- **Avoid jargon and overly technical language** - explain complex concepts simply
- **Natural speech rhythm** - write like people talk, not like reading a dictionary
- **Punchy sentences** - shorter sentences for pacing and comprehension
- **Cut ruthlessly** - if a phrase doesn't advance the story, delete it

**Example of bloated vs. concise:**
- ❌ Bloated: "One of the most interesting and notable things to consider about this particular scenario is the fact that the character has been involved in..."
- ✅ Concise: "The character's involvement reveals..."

---

## Script Structure (7-9 Lines)

- **Total script length: 7-9 spoken lines** (typical YouTube Shorts/TikTok Reels 15-60 second format)
- **Each line advances narrative** - no throwaway or filler lines
- **Clear beginning** - establish topic/hook
- **Middle** - develop idea, present evidence, tell story
- **End** - resolution, payoff, or strong conclusion
- **Logical information flow** - ideas build on each other
- **Line breaks for pacing** - visual representation of speaker pause/breath

**Example structure:**
1. Hook/Question
2. Context/Setup
3. Detail/Evidence
4. Escalation/Complication
5. Resolution/Answer
6. Implication/Meaning
7. Call-to-Action

---

## Language & Tone

- **Conversational and relatable** - speak to the viewer as peer, not lecturer
- **Active voice preferred** - "This changes everything" vs. "Everything is changed by this"
- **Vivid, specific language** - concrete details over abstractions
- **Avoid clichés and overused phrases** - "As we all know..." "It goes without saying..." - no
- **Platform-appropriate tone** - YouTube Shorts = casual but informed, serious but entertaining
- **Personality-driven delivery** - unique voice, not generic script voice
- **Emphasis and inflection implied** - strategic caps or punctuation for vocal performance

**Examples of vivid language:**
- ❌ Generic: "The city is pretty big and impressive"
- ✅ Vivid: "Vice City sprawls across the coast like a neon-soaked sprawl of ambition and danger"

---

## Content Requirements

✅ **INCLUDE:**
- Hook in first sentence
- Verified factual information
- Specific examples or details
- Character/location context
- Clear narrative arc
- Strong ending

❌ **DO NOT INCLUDE:**
- Speculation presented as fact
- Marketing or promotional language
- Clickbait or sensationalism ("SHOCKING!" "NOBODY KNOWS!")
- Personal opinions stated as universal truth
- Unverified claims
- Filler words ("like," "basically," "you know")
- Repeated information
- Tangential stories

---

## Ending & Call-to-Action

- **Strong conclusion that resolves setup** - payoff the hook, answer the question
- **Clear call-to-action (CTA)** - tell viewer what to do next
- **CTA options**:
  - Subscribe for more content
  - Follow for daily updates
  - Like and share
  - Comment your theory
  - Check the link in bio
  - Join the community
- **Tease next content if appropriate** - "Next video we explore..." to drive series engagement
- **Leaves viewer wanting more** - end on intrigue or promise
- **CTA must be natural** - not forced or awkward, flows from script

**Example ending:**
- Hook: "This detail changes EVERYTHING"
- Body: [explanation]
- Resolution: "And that's why it matters"
- CTA: "Follow for more GTA 6 breakdowns."

## Platform Optimization

### YouTube Shorts (15-60 seconds)
- Total script: 7-9 lines
- Pacing: Snappy, fast cuts support narration
- Silence/Music: 2-3 seconds of visual storytelling without narration
- Retention: Strongest hook in first 3 seconds
- End card: CTA in final 2 seconds

### TikTok Reels (15-60 seconds)
- Total script: 7-9 lines
- Trending sounds: Match narration to trending audio if available
- Text overlay: Keep on-screen text minimal (text in script, not image)
- Transition points: Fast cuts between scenes
- Engagement: Question hooks perform well

### Instagram Reels (15-90 seconds)
- Total script: 10-12 lines (slightly longer format)
- Captions: Script supports text overlay captions
- Hashtags: Topic-related hashtags in comments
- Series: Consistent format helps algorithm
- Swipe-up: Link in bio for engagement

## Writing Process Checklist

Before finalizing any script:

- [ ] Hook is compelling and immediate
- [ ] Every claim is verified or labeled as speculation
- [ ] No filler words or phrases
- [ ] 7-9 lines (or platform-appropriate length)
- [ ] Conversational, natural language
- [ ] Active voice used
- [ ] Specific, vivid details included
- [ ] Story has clear arc (beginning/middle/end)
- [ ] Ending resolves setup
- [ ] CTA is natural and clear
- [ ] No marketing language
- [ ] No unverified claims
- [ ] Works without sound (implied visuals)
- [ ] Paces well for platform (15-60 seconds)

## Common Script Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **Opening with context**: "So as you know, in video games..."  
✅ **Open with hook**: "This changes EVERYTHING about Jason's character."

❌ **Hedging language**: "Possibly, maybe, perhaps, arguably..."  
✅ **Confident statements**: "This reveals..." "The evidence shows..."

❌ **Repeating information**: "And to repeat what I just said..."  
✅ **Building on ideas**: "And what's more..." "This implies..."

❌ **Generic descriptions**: "The city is cool and interesting"  
✅ **Specific details**: "Neon-soaked streets, tropical humidity, crime on every corner"

❌ **Long setup**: [3 sentences of context before the actual point]  
✅ **Quick setup**: [1 sentence establishes context, immediately into payload]

❌ **Weak ending**: "Anyway, that's what I think."  
✅ **Strong ending**: "And that's why this moment matters. Follow for more breakdowns."

## Tips for Script Excellence

1. **Read aloud** - does it sound natural? Would you say this in real life?
2. **Cut 20%** - find the fat and remove it without losing meaning
3. **Replace weak verbs** - "is," "has," "does" → "reveals," "transforms," "demands"
4. **Show, don't tell** - "Jason is determined" → "Jason never walks away from a fight"
5. **Start with the end** - know the payoff before writing the setup
6. **One idea per line** - easier to follow, faster pacing
7. **Vary line length** - short sentences for punch, longer for explanation
8. **Remove qualifiers** - "kind of," "sort of," "basically" dilute impact
9. **Use specific numbers** - "Over 5 million views" vs. "Tons of people"
10. **Trust the visuals** - narration doesn't need to explain every detail
